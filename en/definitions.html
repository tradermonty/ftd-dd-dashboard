Definitions

The judgments on this site are the claude-trading-skills code vendored into this project. The figures below are read from that code and its settings.

These are not IBD's official FTD or DD calls. IBD's reading of the market is a human judgment; this site applies the skill's mechanical definitions to the past.

How an FTD is judged

  • Prior decline: a swing low after a fall of 3% or more from a recent high, with at least 3 down days. The reference high is searched up to 40 bars back.
  • Rally day 1: the first day after the swing low that closes up on the previous day, or closes in the top half of its own day's range. A close below the swing-low close at any point resets the rally attempt. On days 2 and 3 the close must not fall below the day-1 intraday low.
  • FTD: on rally day 4 to 10, a gain of at least 1.25% on volume above the previous day.
  • Gains of 1.5% or more are rated recommended and 2% or more strong. The minimum is what the judgment uses.
  • Window: the state is judged on the last 60 bars only; the replay passes 80 bars each day.
  • Invalidation: a close below the FTD-day low. This site counts invalidations within 60 sessions of the FTD.

How a Distribution Day is judged

  • DD: a change of -0.2% or lower from the previous close, on volume above the previous day.
  • Expiry: the DD day counts as 0 and the DD drops out when the age exceeds 25, that is on the 26th session.
  • Invalidation: a DD drops out before expiry if the intraday high rises 5% above the DD-day close.
  • d5, d15 and d25 count the active DDs among today plus the previous 5, 15 and 25 sessions. For example d25 covers 26 sessions: today and the 25 before it.

How this differs from IBD in practice

  • IBD decides by human judgment whether the market is in a correction. This skill decides mechanically with the prior-decline definition above.
  • The gain needed for an FTD differs between sources. The skill's minimum is given above.
  • The decline threshold and the expiry rule for DDs also vary in detail between IBD articles. This site fixes them to the definition above.
  • IBD watches the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite. This site uses ^GSPC and ^IXIC, but the skills take different inputs: the FTD skill uses ^GSPC and QQQ, and the DD skill defaults to QQQ and SPY. QQQ is an ETF that tracks the Nasdaq-100, a different basket from the Nasdaq Composite, and its share volume is not the index volume. Both rules use volume, so the same day can be judged differently.
  • With this definition, 472 FTDs were found across the two indices between 2000-01-03 and 2026-11-25. They were not checked against IBD's own list.

This share is the base rate. If a risk threshold sits close to this level, that level is not a rare warning; it only says the DD count is as high as usual. Compare a risk level with these shares before treating it as a signal.

Detection date and point-in-time judgment

  • Every day's judgment uses only data up to that day. The last 80 bars go to the FTD logic and the last 85 bars to the DD logic, and everything is recomputed from scratch each day.
  • ftd_date is the day designated as the FTD; detected_on is the first day of the replay on which that FTD was visible. If the window moves and an FTD appears later, the two dates differ.
  • In this replay the largest gap between ftd_date and detected_on was 0 sessions, across 472 FTDs.
  • Returns afterwards are measured from the detected_on close. Measuring from ftd_date would use information that was not yet visible. The return measured from ftd_date is kept in separate columns for reference.
  • The replay starts on 2000-01-03. FTDs dated before that cannot be detected point in time, so they are left out of the list.
